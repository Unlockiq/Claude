**Verdict:** A strong paper. All 45 keyed answers check out against the figures. One figure must be fixed (Q14: each cherry emoji shows two cherries, so the counts fail if a child counts single cherries). The other changes are small wording and figure fixes.

## Errors (must fix)

| Q | Problem | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| 14 | Each cherry emoji is a **pair** of cherries (two fruits on joined stems). If a child counts single cherries, the plates hold 10, 10 and 6. That is already more than 5 on every plate, so the question has no answer. The key (2) only works if a child counts the emoji, not the cherries. | Use a single-fruit icon (one cherry, or an apple or orange) with 5, 5 and 3 on the plates. ## Language & clarity

| Q | Current wording | Issue | Suggested rewrite |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "Look at the picture. The ducks walk in pairs. How many ducks are there?" | "Walk in pairs" does not help solve the question, and it pulls toward distractor (A) 8, the number of pairs. The solution talks about rows, not pairs. | "Look at the picture. How many ducks are there?" (Or keep pairs on purpose: "The ducks walk in pairs. Count the ducks. How many ducks are there?") Solution: "There are 8 ducks in each row. 8 and 8 more make 16." |
| 10 | "Look at the two digit cards of Kiara. She uses both cards to make a number." | "Two digit cards of Kiara" is awkward, and it can be read as "two-digit cards". | "Kiara has two digit cards. She uses both cards to make a number. What is the greatest number she can make?" |
| 19 | "A plant nursery has 12 saplings. 7 more saplings arrive. Then 5 saplings are sold." | A sentence starts with a numeral ("7 more…"). "Plant nursery" and "saplings" are hard words for Class 1. | "A plant shop has 12 small plants. Then 7 more small plants arrive. After that, 5 plants are sold. How many plants does the shop have now?" |
| 33 | "Chitra gives a sapling plant food on a Tuesday…" | "Sapling" and "plant food" are hard to read together ("a sapling plant food"). | "Chitra feeds her small plant on a Tuesday. She feeds it again 7 days later. On which day is that?" |
| 43 | "The gaps grow: 1 day, 2 days, 3 days." | "Gap" is unclear. A child may count the days *in between* (Mon→Tue has 0 days in between, Tue→Thu has 1, Thu→Sun has 2). The given numbers then do not fit, and the child may answer Wednesday (3 days in between after Sunday). | "Rosa plays badminton on Monday, Tuesday, Thursday and Sunday. She plays again 1 day later, then 2 days later, then 3 days later. Next she will play 4 days later. On which day does she play next?" (Or, to keep it harder, leave out the last sentence and write "Each time she waits one more day.") |
| 37 | "The three shapes at the top belong together: they have the same number of corners." | A colon in a Class 1 question. Use two short sentences. | "The three shapes at the top belong together. They have the same number of corners. Which picture shows one more shape that belongs with them?" |
| 41 | "The middle number has rubbed off." | Reads better in the passive voice for children. | "This piece is cut from a 1 to 100 number chart. The middle number is rubbed off. First find it. Then find the number in the ? box below it." |
| Sol. 18, 19, 20, 21, 24, 40 | e.g. "9 copies are left.", "2 planks are there…", "77, 55 and 99 are…" | Solution sentences start with a numeral (style rule). | e.g. Q20: "There are 9 copies left. 13 − 9 = 4…"; Q24: "There are 2 planks already, so…"; Q40: "The numbers 77, 55 and 99…"; Q18: "We take away: 19 − 13 = 6." |
| Cover | "All figures are not necessarily drawn to scale unless stated." | Bad logic ("all … not" means none are to scale), and "unless stated" adds nothing. It is also out of place for counting pictures, which are exact. | "Pictures may not be drawn to scale." (Or delete it for Class 1.) |
| Cover | "Look at the picture or read the question carefully." | "Or" suggests a child can do only one of them. | "Read each question and look at its picture carefully." |
